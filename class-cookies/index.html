Class Cookies

Classroom games and tools for English teachers, made for one projector or TV and no student devices. Every game is free to play in full with no sign-in. A subscription adds more content in most games and more of My Classroom.

What's in it

Everything a teacher puts on the screen

Five games

59 Seconds, Taco Trivia, Mango Mafia, Super Mushroom Man and Cookie Code. Each runs on its own address under classcookies.com and is free to play in full; a subscription adds more decks, packs or roles in four of them.

My Classroom

Class lists with students, avatars and scores, comments on students, word and sentence lists, and a calendar. Free accounts keep two classes; the scoreboard, team decider and name spinner can load a class.

Free tools

Eight tools that need no account: classroom timer, stopwatch, clock, a name spinner ("Let the Donut Decide"), team decider, coin flip, scoreboard and whiteboard.

The Staffroom

43 free worksheets anyone can download, and a discussion forum where subscribed teachers post, reply and save collections. Posts can be reported, and an admin moderates them.

Pricing. $4.99 a month or $49.99 a year. Public sign-up is not open yet: the pricing page says "Subscriptions open soon."

Architecture

Six apps that feel like one

The platform and each game are separate repositories and separate deploys, so one can change without risking the others. They share two things: a sign-in, and one answer to "has this teacher subscribed?"

The database decides, not the page

The platform database has 25 tables, and row-level security is switched on for every one: 90 policies say who may read or change each row. Whether a teacher has paid is one function, is_subscribed(), used inside those policies, so a page that forgets a check cannot leak or unlock anything.

Billing kept narrow

Paddle is the merchant of record. Its signed webhooks are the only thing that writes a subscription, through one database function that applies each event once. The privileged database key is used in exactly two places: that billing function and the short-lived download links for worksheets.

Data and privacy

Built around classroom data

  • A class list holds a name, an avatar and an optional gender for each student: no emails, birthdays or contact details. Comments on students are the most sensitive rows, readable only by the teacher who owns the class.
  • When a teacher opens Mango Mafia with a class, only the class's id travels in the link, never a name; the game reads the names with the teacher's own session.
  • The platform runs no analytics, tag manager or tracking script, and calls no AI service; automated tests fail if either is added.
  • A daily backup copies the database through a read-only role, encrypts it with gpg to a key the backup machine cannot decrypt, and keeps copies off that machine. A full restore was tested on 2 October 2026.
  • Free accounts can always read and delete their classes, even past the free limit; only adding and editing are limited.
  • No real teacher or student data appears on this site.
